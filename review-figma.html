Why it earns a place

Figma keeps sketches, layouts, prototypes, feedback, and design-system decisions in a shared place. That is useful when the visual direction is still being negotiated and the people who need to agree are not in the same room.

It also supports a slower kind of collaboration: leave context beside the design, show the alternatives, and make the decision visible. That can be more valuable than producing a polished image immediately.

Where the trade-offs appear

Figma can be excessive for a single social graphic or a small brand refresh. If the work does not need shared interface thinking, a simpler visual tool may keep the process lighter.

Pricing is seat-based and the official page now shows several seat types. The right plan depends on whether a person needs full design access, developer handoff, or lighter collaboration. Do not price every collaborator as a full seat by accident.

Stackwise note

Choose the tool that matches the conversation. A product decision needs collaboration; a simple deliverable may only need speed.
