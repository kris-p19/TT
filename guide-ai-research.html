Workflow guide

How to build an AI research workflow without losing the source.

The goal is not to let AI search for you. It is to create a small loop where questions become evidence, evidence becomes a decision, and the decision stays connected to the work it affects.

By Stackwise EditorialSeptember 23, 2026·7 min read

The loop

Question → evidence → decision → record.

Keep the loop small enough that you can repeat it every week.

See the AI review →

Start with a decision, not a topic

“Research AI tools” is too broad to finish. “Which tool should I use to organize a five-project week?” gives the research a boundary. Write the decision you need to make before opening an AI assistant or a browser tab.

A useful research question has a subject, a constraint, and an output. For example: “Which workspace can hold client notes, project tasks, and a simple weekly review for a solo consultant?”

Collect evidence in a fixed place

Keep the source, the date, and the reason you saved it together. A short note is more valuable than a long highlight you will never revisit. If you are using an AI assistant, ask it to separate what came from the source from what is an inference.

This is where a flexible workspace helps. Notion or a simple document can hold the research log, but the important part is the habit, not the app.

Ask for a decision brief

After gathering a few sources, ask the assistant to produce a decision brief: what is known, what is uncertain, what trade-off matters most, and what should be tested next. Keep the output close to the original evidence.

Do not let the assistant hide uncertainty. A confident paragraph can be more dangerous than an obviously incomplete one because it is easier to reuse.

Record the decision

Write down what you chose, what you rejected, and the condition that would make you change your mind. This is the part that turns research into a reusable asset for the business.

A decision record can be short: date, question, choice, reason, next review. Future you will be grateful for the context when the price or the workflow changes.

Stackwise note

A research workflow is successful when the next person—or your future self—can understand why the decision was made.
