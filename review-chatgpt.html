ChatGPT review: a strong first layer for independent work.

ChatGPT is most useful when the work is still taking shape: a rough brief needs structure, a research question needs a first pass, or a blank page needs a useful starting point.

The short answer

Use ChatGPT to get from a vague thought to a clear next move—not as the final authority on your work.

See the workflow →

Why it earns a place

The biggest advantage is low friction. You can move from a question to a useful outline without first learning a complicated interface or building a project system. That makes it a good companion for a freelancer who needs to think before committing to a system.

It is also useful for translation between formats. A messy meeting note can become a client brief; a long article can become a checklist; a rough idea can become several angles to explore. The value is in the transformation, not the novelty of the interface.

Where the trade-offs appear

A fast answer is not automatically a correct answer. Give it the right source material, define the audience, and state what a good output looks like. If the context is missing, the tool will fill the gap with plausible assumptions.

It also works best as part of a stack rather than as the whole stack. Keep final decisions, source material, and project state somewhere you can inspect and return to. Use the assistant to move faster, then use your own process to decide what deserves to survive.

Treat AI output as a first draft, not a source of truth. Your judgment is the part that makes the workflow yours.
