Workflow guide

A repeatable visual brief for faster, better design.

The fastest way to improve a visual workflow is not to add more inspiration. It is to give every design request the same useful context.

By Stackwise EditorialSeptember 23, 2026·6 min read

The brief

Audience, purpose, format, constraints, next action.

See the Canva review →

Name the audience before the style

A visual can look polished and still fail because it speaks to the wrong person. Start with who will see it, what they need to understand, and what they should do next.

A good audience line is concrete: “A busy founder scanning a phone before a client call” is more useful than “modern professionals.”

Give the tool a job

Do not ask for a design when you mean a social announcement, a product explanation, or a client presentation. State the job and the format. “One square image for an announcement with the offer and date” gives the tool a better boundary than “make something about our offer.”

If the work is a product interface rather than a marketing asset, use a product-focused tool such as Figma. If it is a fast, approachable visual, Canva is often the lighter starting point.

Set constraints before generating

List the dimensions, must-use assets, words that cannot change, and the feeling the work should create. Constraints are not a limitation; they are how you keep the first version from wandering.

Keep a small set of reusable brand rules: one or two type styles, a limited palette, spacing, and a clear image treatment. Save them once and reference them in every brief.

Review the first version as a draft

The first output is a conversation starter. Check whether the hierarchy works, whether the message is understandable, and whether the visual belongs to the brand. Then ask for one specific revision instead of asking for “something better.”

A repeatable workflow is one that makes the next version faster to explain. The tool matters less than the shared brief.

The small test

If two people use your brief, do they make work that feels related?

That is the sign of a visual system, not a folder of random templates.
